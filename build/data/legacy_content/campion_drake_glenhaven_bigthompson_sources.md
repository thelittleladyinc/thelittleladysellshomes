## 1. CAMPION, COLORADO

### Basic geography
- Campion is an **unincorporated community** in southeastern **Larimer County, Colorado** — it has no municipal government ([Larimer County government — Cities & Towns in Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/about/cities-towns); [Wikipedia: Campion, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campion,_Colorado); [Berthoud Surveyor, June 2025](https://berthoudsurveyor.com/berthoud-board-talks-growth-grants-and-the-garden-spot/)).
- It was formerly a **census-designated place (CDP)** in the 2000 Census, but it is not currently tracked as an active CDP ([Wikipedia: Campion, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campion,_Colorado)).
- Campion sits **between Loveland and Berthoud along U.S. Route 287** ([Wikipedia: Campion, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campion,_Colorado)).
- Approximate distances: Campion is about **2.7 miles north of Berthoud** and **3.4 miles south of Loveland** ([HomeTownLocator: Campion populated place profile](https://colorado.hometownlocator.com/co/larimer/campion.cfm)). A driving-route estimate between Loveland and Berthoud (which Campion sits between) is roughly **7 miles / 12 minutes** ([Trippy: Loveland to Berthoud distance](https://www.trippy.com/distance/Loveland-CO-to-Berthoud)).
- Fort Collins is roughly 15 miles / 20–30 minutes from Loveland via US-287 ([TheUSATrip: Loveland to Fort Collins](https://www.theusatrip.com/trip/loveland-to-fort-collins-travel); [Distance-Cities: Fort Collins to Loveland](https://www.distance-cities.com/distance-fort-collins-co-to-loveland-co); [Travelmath: Loveland to Fort Collins](https://www.travelmath.com/driving-time/from/Loveland,+CO/to/Fort+Collins,+CO)) — Campion, being just a few miles south of Loveland, would add roughly 5–10 minutes to a Fort Collins commute.

### Population / character
- The population of the former Campion CDP was **1,832 at the 2000 Census** (up from 1,692 in 1990, +8.3%) ([Wikipedia: Campion, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campion,_Colorado)). No official current population count is confirmed for present-day Campion as an active CDP — **UNVERIFIED — do not use** any post-2000 population figure for Campion without a fresh census/ACS source.
- Character: a rural-residential/suburbanizing unincorporated community; local coverage in 2024–2025 describes active development pressure, including a proposal to annex adjacent land into the Town of Berthoud, which drew opposition from Campion residents concerned about loss of community character and overdevelopment ([Berthoud Surveyor, Dec. 2024](https://berthoudsurveyor.com/campion-annexation-proposal-draws-community-ire/); [Berthoud Surveyor, June 2025](https://berthoudsurveyor.com/berthoud-board-talks-growth-grants-and-the-garden-spot/)).

### School district
- Campion falls within the **Thompson School District R2-J**, headquartered in Loveland, which covers Loveland, Berthoud, a southern portion of Fort Collins, and parts of Windsor, Johnstown, and unincorporated Larimer, Weld, and Boulder counties ([Wikipedia: Thompson School District R2-J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thompson_School_District_R2-J); [Colorado Dept. of Education — Thompson R2-J profile](https://cde.state.co.us/schoolview/financialtransparency/organizations/1560)). Specific boundary schools for any given Campion address should be confirmed via TSD's official **School Locator** tool ([Thompson School District — School Locator](https://www.tsd.org/family/school-locator)) since exact attendance zones vary by street address.

### Notable feature: Campion Academy
- **Campion Academy** is a private Seventh-day Adventist Christian **boarding high school** (grades 9–12) located in Campion/Loveland, founded in **1907** ([Wikipedia: Campion Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campion_Academy); [Campion Academy official site](https://www.campion.net/about.html)).
- Address: 300 42nd St SW, Loveland, CO 80537; operated by the Rocky Mountain Conference of Seventh-day Adventists ([Campion Academy official site](https://www.campion.net/); [Adventist Yearbook entity record](https://www.adventistyearbook.org/entity?EntityID=17411)).
- Per the school's own student handbook, the academy is located **"fifty miles north of Denver, three miles south of Loveland, and 20 miles east of the Rocky Mountains, within view of Long's Peak"** ([Campion Academy Student Handbook 2021 PDF](https://www.campion.net/uploads/5/6/4/8/56483391/student_handbook_2021_final.pdf)).
- The academy reported an enrollment of approximately 115 students as of its Wikipedia profile ([Wikipedia: Campion Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campion_Academy)); a 2022 school announcement cited a graduating class of 47 seniors, described as the largest in 11 years ([Rocky Mountain Conference SDA blog, June 2022](https://www.rmcsda.org/campion-academy-celebrates-a-high-achieving-class/)).
- Land near Campion Academy has recently been the subject of a development proposal (a 198-unit residential project) that drew opposition from area residents, per 2025 local reporting ([Berthoud Surveyor, June 2025](https://berthoudsurveyor.com/berthoud-board-talks-growth-grants-and-the-garden-spot/)).

### Real estate character
- Campion is unincorporated, so property owners rely on Larimer County (not a municipal government) for zoning and services ([Berthoud Surveyor, June 2025](https://berthoudsurveyor.com/berthoud-board-talks-growth-grants-and-the-garden-spot/)).
- **UNVERIFIED — do not use**: No independently sourced data was found on well/septic prevalence specific to Campion; unlike the canyon communities, Campion is closer to Loveland/Berthoud municipal infrastructure and may have more properties on municipal or district water/sewer. This should be confirmed property-by-property before publishing any blanket claim.
- Wildfire risk in Campion is materially lower than in the canyon communities since it sits on the plains/foothill transition rather than in forested mountain terrain — but no specific wildfire-zone designation for Campion was located in this research. **UNVERIFIED — do not use** any specific wildfire-risk-tier claim for Campion without checking the Larimer County Wildfire Hazard Area map ([Larimer County Wildfire Maps](https://www.larimer.gov/emergency/fires/maps)).

### Zip code
- **80537** (Loveland) ([Wikipedia: Campion, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campion,_Colorado); [Campion Academy official site](https://www.campion.net/uploads/5/6/4/8/56483391/student_handbook_2021_final.pdf)).

## 2. DRAKE, COLORADO

### Basic geography
- Drake is an **unincorporated community and U.S. Post Office** located in and governed by **Larimer County, Colorado** ([Wikipedia: Drake, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake,_Colorado); [Larimer County — Cities & Towns](https://www.larimer.gov/about/cities-towns)).
- It sits along **U.S. Highway 34 in the Big Thompson Canyon**, between Estes Park and Loveland, near Rocky Mountain National Park ([Wikipedia: Drake, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake,_Colorado)). Elevation is **6,161–6,162 feet** ([Wikipedia: Drake, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake,_Colorado)).
- Drake is roughly **10.4 miles from Estes Park** ([AllTrips: Big Thompson Canyon scenic drive](https://www.allestespark.com/scenic_drives/big_thompson_canyon.php)) and about **20 minutes from Estes Park / 40 minutes from Fort Collins** by car per a local real-estate source ([Richardson Team Realty: Drake community page](https://richardsonteamrealty.com/communities/drake/)).
- The Larimer County parks department places **Sleepy Hollow Park in Drake roughly 8.3 miles east of Estes Park and 22 miles west of Loveland** ([Larimer County — Big Thompson Parks](https://www.larimer.gov/naturalresources/parks/big-thompson)).
- The full Loveland–Estes Park drive via US-34 through Big Thompson Canyon is commonly cited as **~30 miles and 45–55 minutes** ([TheUSATrip: Loveland to Estes Park](https://www.theusatrip.com/trip/loveland-to-estes-park-travel); [Wanderlog: Loveland to Estes Park](https://wanderlog.com/drive/between/58470/58310/loveland-to-estes-park-drive); [Travelmath: Loveland to Estes Park](https://www.travelmath.com/distance/from/Loveland,+CO/to/Estes+Park,+CO); [Visit Estes Park — Loveland to Estes Park itinerary](https://www.visitestespark.com/blog/post/one-hour-to-the-mountains-a-loveland-to-estes-park-itinerary/)), placing Drake roughly at the midpoint to two-thirds point of that drive.

### Population / character
- The **2010 ZIP-code-area population of Drake was 1,010** ({{sources below}}) ([Wikipedia: Drake, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake,_Colorado); [Kiddle: Drake, Colorado](https://kids.kiddle.co/Drake,_Colorado); [The Diggings: Drake, Colorado](https://thediggings.com/places/co069-99927387)).
- A local Estes Park real-estate firm describes Drake as having "a population of approximately 1,000" ([The Thompson Group / estesparkhome.com: Drake community page](https://estesparkhome.com/communities/drake/)). One travel-guide source separately describes Drake's population as "about 200 people," a figure that conflicts with the ZIP-code population count above — **flagging this discrepancy**: the ~200 figure may refer to year-round core Drake residents versus the wider 80515 ZIP-code area population of ~1,010. Cite each: ([AllTrips: Big Thompson Canyon](https://www.allestespark.com/scenic_drives/big_thompson_canyon.php)) vs. ([Wikipedia: Drake, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake,_Colorado)).
- Character: a small canyon/river community, described by Colorado's official tourism site as maintaining "small-town appeal" with bed and breakfasts and shops, serving as a gateway to Rocky Mountain National Park ([Colorado.com: Drake](https://www.colorado.com/cities-and-towns/drake)). Most homes are located along the river or on the shoulders of Storm Mountain near the junction of US-34 and County Road 43 ([Richardson Team Realty: Drake](https://richardsonteamrealty.com/communities/drake/)).

### School district
- Drake falls within the **Estes Park School District R-3**, headquartered in Estes Park. The district's own materials state it "covers boundaries within the Estes Valley, including areas such as Glen Haven, Drake, Allenspark and Pinewood Springs" ([Estes Park School District — Our District](https://www.estesschools.org/our-district)).
- The district operates 3 schools serving roughly 945–971 total students as of recent Colorado Department of Education data ([CDE SchoolView — Estes Park R-3 profile](https://www.cde.state.co.us/schoolview/explore/profile/1570); [NCES district search](https://nces.ed.gov/ccd/districtsearch/district_list.asp?Search=1&County=LARIMER&ID2=0803810&details=)).
- Some Drake-area properties are marketed as zoned to **Big Thompson Elementary School** (part of Thompson School District, located down-canyon near Loveland), per a specific property listing ([Homes.com listing: 12615 County Road 43, Drake](https://www.homes.com/property/12615-county-road-43-drake-co/1nfvyhskts7ht/)) — this suggests school assignment in the canyon can depend on the specific address/district boundary, and buyers should verify via the relevant district's locator tool rather than assume one district covers 100% of the canyon.

### Big Thompson River, 1976 flood, 2013 flood, Cameron Peak Fire
**Big Thompson River:** The Big Thompson River runs through Drake along US-34; it originates in Rocky Mountain National Park and flows east through the canyon to Loveland and beyond ([Wikipedia: Big Thompson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Thompson_River); [Komoot: Big Thompson River](https://www.komoot.com/highlight/6245601)).

**1976 Big Thompson Flood — death toll:** Sources report the death toll as **143 or 144**, with **5 people never found/missing**. This is described consistently as Colorado's deadliest natural disaster in recorded history. Cited figures:
- **144 deaths, 5 missing, >250 injuries** — [Wikipedia: 1976 Big Thompson River flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Big_Thompson_River_flood); [The Coloradoan, July 2016](https://www.coloradoan.com/story/news/2016/07/29/big-thompson-flood-killed-scores/87524858/); [Denver Post, July 2026 (50th anniversary)](https://www.denverpost.com/2026/07/27/big-thompson-flood-50th-anniversary-advances/); [Denver Gazette, July 2026](https://www.denvergazette.com/2026/07/27/looking-back-at-the-flood-that-claimed-144-lives-in-colorado-50-years-later/); [Colorado National Guard, 2026](https://co.ng.mil/News/Archives/Article/4561952/the-night-the-sky-collapsed-the-1976-big-thompson-canyon-flood/); [Rocky Mountain PBS, 2026](https://www.rmpbs.org/news/history/big-thompson-flood-50-years-later/); [Denver7, 2023](https://www.denver7.com/about/70-years-of-denver7/how-the-historic-big-thompson-canyon-flood-of-1976-unfolded-on-denver7-news); [CBS8, 2015 retrospective](https://www.cbs8.com/article/weather/remembering-big-thompson-flood-39-years-later/73-130237511).
- **139 deaths, 5 missing** (official USGS scientific publication) — [USGS Professional Paper 1115 A/B](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/pp1115AB).
- **143 deaths** (USGS news release and Wikipedia's Big Thompson River article) — [USGS news release, 2016](https://www.usgs.gov/news/state-news-release/big-thompson-canyon-floods-1976-and-2013-tale-two-deadly-and-destructive); [Wikipedia: Big Thompson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Thompson_River).
- The USGS 30-years-later fact sheet states **144 deaths, 250 injuries, more than 800 people evacuated by helicopter, 418 homes/businesses destroyed, and over $35 million in 1976-dollar damages** — [USGS Fact Sheet 2006-3095](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2006/3095/).
- **Best-supported figure for publication: 144 deaths (5 of whom were never found), ~250 injuries** — this is the figure used by the majority of sources including Wikipedia, the Coloradoan, Denver Post, Denver Gazette, and Colorado National Guard's 50th-anniversary retrospectives. The USGS's own primary 1976-era publication (Professional Paper 1115A/B) states 139 deaths — cite this range/discrepancy if precision is challenged.
- Damage estimate: **more than $35 million in 1977 dollars** (~$150–234 million in current dollars depending on the inflation-adjustment source and year cited) — [USGS Fact Sheet 2006-3095](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2006/3095/); [Denver Post, 2026](https://www.denverpost.com/2026/07/27/big-thompson-flood-50th-anniversary-advances/) (states $234 million in today's dollars); [Colorado National Guard](https://co.ng.mil/News/Archives/Article/4561952/the-night-the-sky-collapsed-the-1976-big-thompson-canyon-flood/) (states ~$180 million today).
- The flood was caused by a stalled thunderstorm dropping **12–14 inches of rain** (with 7.5 inches falling in a single hour) over the Big Thompson watershed on the evening of **July 31, 1976** — Colorado's centennial ([USGS Fact Sheet 2006-3095](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2006/3095/); [Wikipedia: 1976 Big Thompson River flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Big_Thompson_River_flood)).
- **Drake specifically:** According to BizWest's 50th-anniversary retrospective, the flood's peak surge struck **Glen Comfort at 8 p.m., Waltonia at 8:30, Drake at 9 p.m., and Cedar Cove at 9:30 p.m.** ([BizWest, July 2026](https://bizwest.com/2026/07/26/disasters-50th-anniversary-brings-flood-of-memories/)). The 1976 flood "impacted most of Cedar Cove, Drake, and Midway" and caused extensive damage in Glen Haven ([Wikipedia: 1976 Big Thompson River flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Big_Thompson_River_flood)). Drake itself was destroyed by the 1976 flood, per a travel feature ([Islands.com: Drake feature](https://www.islands.com/2139552/drake-sandwiched-between-rocky-mountain-national-park-fort-collins-colorado-quaint-quiet-gateway/)).

**2013 Colorado floods (September 2013):**
- Statewide death toll for the entire 2013 event is most commonly cited as **8 or 9 deaths** (some early reports cited up to 10), with sources differing slightly:
  - **8 deaths statewide** — [Wikipedia: 2013 Colorado floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Colorado_floods); [Colorado Encyclopedia](https://coloradoencyclopedia.org/article/september-2013-floods); [AMS Journal — The Great Colorado Flood of September 2013](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/bams/96/9/bams-d-13-00241.1.xml).
  - **9 deaths statewide** — [Colorado Newsline, 2023](https://coloradonewsline.com/2023/09/05/almost-unimaginable-the-2013-colorado-flood-10-years-later/); [KUNC, 2023](https://www.kunc.org/news/2023-09-11/after-devastating-2013-floods-glen-haven-locals-gather-at-rebuilt-town-hall-to-mark-anniversary); [The Coloradoan, 2022](https://www.coloradoan.com/story/news/2022/09/09/colorado-2013-flood-anniversary-look-back-estes-park-devastation/67929925007/); [AOL/AccuWeather retrospective, 2025](https://www.aol.com/articles/date-billion-dollar-colorado-flood-100000768.html).
  - **10 deaths statewide** — [Colorado DHSEM After Action Report, 2013 floods](https://dhsem.colorado.gov/sites/dhsem/files/documents/Colorado%202013%20Floods%20and%20Black%20Forest%20Fire_%20AAR_IP_Final_6_30_15%20(1).pdf).
  - **Big Thompson Canyon specifically:** Two women from the Cedar Cove neighborhood of Big Thompson Canyon — Patty Goodwine (60) and Evelyn Starner (79) — were reported missing and presumed dead, swept away with their homes ([North Forty News, Sept. 2013](https://northfortynews.com/category/uncategorized/first-fire-now-flood-the-great-flood-of-2013/); [Jacobs/NAEP historic preservation report](https://visualmedia.jacobs.com/NAEP/pdf/Panel_3/Historic%20Preservation%20-%20The%20River%20and%20the%20Road.pdf) confirms "two deaths" in the Big Thompson River/North Fork basins specifically, and a separate Big Thompson River Restoration Master Plan states "this flood caused two deaths" in the Big Thompson basin ([Big Thompson Watershed Coalition Master Plan PDF](https://bigthompson.co/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/BTWC_Master_Plan.pdf)).
- Damage: statewide damage estimates range from **$2 billion to nearly $4 billion**, with roughly **1,500–1,882 homes destroyed statewide** and **200 businesses destroyed in Larimer County alone**; **1,120 square miles flooded in Larimer County**; approximately **4,500 businesses and 2,000+ homes impacted** in Larimer County ([Wikipedia: 2013 Colorado floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Colorado_floods); [North Forty News](https://northfortynews.com/category/uncategorized/first-fire-now-flood-the-great-flood-of-2013/); [Colorado DHSEM After Action Report](https://dhsem.colorado.gov/sites/dhsem/files/documents/Colorado%202013%20Floods%20and%20Black%20Forest%20Fire_%20AAR_IP_Final_6_30_15%20(1).pdf)).
- **Road access:** "Extensive road damage in Big Thompson Canyon completely cut off road access to the communities of Drake, Glen Haven, and Cedar [Park/Cove]" ([Wikipedia: 2013 Colorado floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Colorado_floods); [Colorado DHSEM report](https://dhsem.colorado.gov/sites/dhsem/files/documents/Colorado%202013%20Floods%20and%20Black%20Forest%20Fire_%20AAR_IP_Final_6_30_15%20(1).pdf)). US 34 was washed out for roughly 9 miles ([Jacobs/NAEP report](https://visualmedia.jacobs.com/NAEP/pdf/Panel_3/Historic%20Preservation%20-%20The%20River%20and%20the%20Road.pdf)) and more than 10 miles of roadway embankment were destroyed according to the project engineering firm Jacobs ([Jacobs: US 34 Big Thompson Canyon Permanent Repair](https://www.jacobs.com/projects/us-34-big-thompson-canyon-permanent-repair)).
- CDOT documented that US 34 in Big Thompson Canyon "was heavily damaged during the 2013 floods, with many homes damaged and over 100 air-lifted evacuations," and that permanent reconstruction cost **$280 million** ([CODOT: US 34 Big Thompson Canyon project archive](https://www.codot.gov/projects/archives/us-34-big-thompson-canyon)). Jacobs cites more than 2,000 people evacuated from the canyon, many by helicopter ([Jacobs project page](https://www.jacobs.com/projects/us-34-big-thompson-canyon-permanent-repair)).
- Temporary repairs reopened US 34 to traffic in both directions on **November 11, 2013**; the full multi-year permanent-repair project (design starting 2014, construction starting 2016) cost **$280 million** total and the highway did not fully reopen to unrestricted traffic until **May 2018** ([CODOT project archive](https://www.codot.gov/projects/archives/us-34-big-thompson-canyon); [Visit Estes Park, May 2018](https://www.visitestespark.com/blog/post/highway-34-now-open-through-big-thompson-canyon/); [CBS News Colorado, 2020 retrospective](https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/big-thompson-canyon-highway-34/)).
- A 46-year-old Drake man whose home washed away in the 2013 flood was initially feared dead but was later found alive ([North Forty News, Sept. 2013](https://northfortynews.com/category/uncategorized/first-fire-now-flood-the-great-flood-of-2013/)).
- In the 2013 flood, thanks to improved warning systems built after 1976, "the death toll in the canyons was in the single digits" compared to 1976 ([BizWest, July 2026](https://bizwest.com/2026/07/26/disasters-50th-anniversary-brings-flood-of-memories/)).

**2020 Cameron Peak Fire — proximity/impact:**
- The Cameron Peak Fire (2020) was Colorado's **largest wildfire on record** at the time, ultimately burning **~208,913 acres** per National Park Service reporting timeframe context, though a Denver7 report from mid-fire cited **187,537 acres** as of mid-October 2020 ([Denver7, Oct. 2020](https://www.denver7.com/news/wildfire/more-evacuations-issued-for-cameron-peak-fire-as-wind-picks-up-Friday-afternoon)) — **note: final acreage should be verified against a definitive post-containment source before publishing an exact total acreage figure.**
- On **October 14–17, 2020**, the fire made a run of roughly 25,000 acres toward the **Big Thompson Canyon and Estes Park** ([Colorado Encyclopedia: Cameron Peak Fire](https://coloradoencyclopedia.org/article/cameron-peak-fire)).
- CDOT closed **US Highway 34 in the Big Thompson Canyon** (from Mall Road in Estes Park to east of the Dam Store) on October 16, 2020, specifically to allow evacuations related to the fire's escalation; evacuations were ordered for Glen Haven, and further evacuations were ordered for "US 34 from Drake to just west of the Dam Store" ([CODOT news release, Oct. 16, 2020](https://www.codot.gov/news/2020/october-2020/us34-firerelatedclosure)).
- Firefighters focused specifically on protecting homes in and around **Drake and Glen Haven**, an area described as having "hundreds of cabins perched on heavily forested slopes and ridges" ([Colorado Sun, Oct. 2020](https://coloradosun.com/2020/10/17/winds-fuel-cameron-peak-fires-growth-toward-fort-collins-more-evacuations-ordered/)).
- **No deaths were reported from the Cameron Peak Fire**; it burned approximately 469 structures (224 of them houses) across its full extent, and forced thousands of evacuations, including over 6,000 residents at its peak across Larimer County communities ([Colorado Encyclopedia: Cameron Peak Fire](https://coloradoencyclopedia.org/article/cameron-peak-fire); [Wikipedia: Cameron Peak fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Peak_fire)).
- By late October 2020, evacuation orders for areas along Larimer County Road 43 northwest of Drake were downgraded to voluntary as snow helped firefighters gain containment ([KUNC, Oct. 29, 2020](https://www.kunc.org/2020-10-29/latest-updates-fire-season-not-over-arapaho-roosevelt-national-forest-remain-closed)).

### Real estate character
- Homes in Drake are commonly on **acreage** with mountain/riverfront settings; example listings include a 35-acre parcel bordering National Forest ([Homes.com listing](https://www.homes.com/property/12615-county-road-43-drake-co/1nfvyhskts7ht/)), riverfront cabins, and vacant land parcels of several acres ([Zillow: Big Thompson River 80517/80515 listings](https://www.zillow.com/estes-park-co-80517/big-thompson-river_att/); [Trulia: 80517 land listings](https://www.trulia.com/CO/Estes_Park/80517/LOT%7CLAND_type/)).
- The community "suffered greatly during the 2013 floods, but as with previous floods, is rebuilding and stronger than ever," per a local real-estate brokerage, which also notes most homes sit along the river or on the shoulders of Storm Mountain ([Richardson Team Realty: Drake](https://richardsonteamrealty.com/communities/drake/)).
- **Wildfire insurance:** Larimer County's mountain/canyon zones, including the Big Thompson Canyon corridor, are widely reported as an area where major national insurance carriers (State Farm, Allstate, USAA, Travelers) have issued non-renewals in recent years due to wildfire risk, pushing homeowners toward surplus-lines carriers or Colorado's new **FAIR Plan** (accepting applications since April 2025) as insurer of last resort ([Colorado Division of Insurance Wildfire Preparedness Guide](https://doi.colorado.gov/sites/doi/files/documents/Colorado%20Property%20and%20Insurance%20Wildfire%20Preparedness%20Guide.pdf); industry-analysis sources describing Larimer County mountain-area premiums in the **$3,200–$7,500/year** range as of 2026 — **flag as secondary/aggregator sourcing, not a government figure**, cite as: [HomeOfferNinja: Colorado Wildfire Insurance Crisis 2026](https://homeofferninja.com/colorado-wildfire-insurance-crisis-2026); [Western Wildfire Defense: Travelers non-renewal Colorado](https://western-wildfire-defense.com/co/travelers-non-renewal/)). Colorado law (C.R.S. §10-4-110.7, per HB23-1174) requires insurers to give **60 days' written notice** before a non-renewal takes effect ([Western Wildfire Defense](https://western-wildfire-defense.com/co/travelers-non-renewal/)).
- Larimer County's 2025 Wildfire Resiliency Code establishes defensible-space and structure-hardening requirements for properties in designated wildland-urban interface areas, which include the Big Thompson Canyon corridor ([Larimer County 2025 Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/2025-wildfire-resiliency-code-w-lc-amendments.pdf)).
- In late 2025, Larimer County was awarded a **$9.856 million federal Community Wildfire Defense Grant** specifically to fund defensible-space mitigation on up to 900 parcels along "the forested landscape corridor along the Big Thompson Canyon," in partnership with the Estes Valley Fire Protection District and the Glen Haven/Retreat community wildfire protection plan ([Larimer County news release, Nov. 25, 2025](https://www.larimer.gov/spotlights/2025/11/25/larimer-county-awarded-98m-grant-protecting-gateway-rockies-wildfire-defense)).
- **Well/septic:** No single authoritative source was found stating "100% of Drake is on well/septic," but given Drake's unincorporated, rural-canyon status with no municipal water/sewer utility mentioned in any source reviewed, well and septic systems are the standard infrastructure for canyon properties — this is consistent with general guidance for unincorporated Colorado mountain communities describing well/septic due diligence steps for buyers ([general mountain buyer guidance: Envision Realty Group — Evergreen wells/septic/fire guide](https://envisionrealtygroupllc.com/blog/evergreen-mountain-buyer-guide-to-wells-septic-and-fire-risk)). **Flag: the specific claim "Drake is 100% well/septic" is UNVERIFIED — do not use without a direct Larimer County Health Department or Environmental Health source confirming the exact percentage.**
- **Road access:** US-34 through Big Thompson Canyon has a documented history of extended winter closures for construction — e.g., a full winter closure from October 2017 through Memorial Day 2018, during which Drake and other canyon residents required CDOT-issued access permits ([CODOT news release, Aug. 2017](https://www.codot.gov/news/2017-news/august/us-34-from-estes-park-to-loveland-to-close-oct-2)); a similar closure occurred from October 2016 through Memorial Day 2017 for post-2013-flood permanent repairs ([CPR, Sept. 2016](https://www.cpr.org/2016/09/29/brace-yourself-northern-colorado-us-34-big-thompson-canyon-closure-is-almost-here/)). As of recent reporting, US-34 is open to all traffic between Loveland and Estes Park, though ongoing maintenance (e.g., rockfall/rock-scaling work) periodically requires brief closures — e.g., scheduled 30-minute closures near Drake for rock-scaling in May 2025 ([Estes Valley Voice, May 2025](https://estesvalleyvoice.com/2025/05/31/big-thompson-canyon-road-work-this-week/); [CODOT project archive, updated Aug. 2026](https://www.codot.gov/projects/archives/us-34-big-thompson-canyon)). Signs along the narrow canyon route warn of flash-flood risk ([AARoads: US 34 Colorado](https://www.aaroads.com/guides/us-034-co)).

Some Drake-area/Big Thompson Canyon properties near the Estes Park side are also listed under ZIP **80517** ([Zillow: 80517 listings including Big Thompson River properties](https://www.zillow.com/estes-park-co-80517/big-thompson-river_att/); [Redfin: 80517 listings](https://www.redfin.com/zipcode/80517)).

## 3. GLEN HAVEN, COLORADO

### Basic geography
- Glen Haven is an **unincorporated community and U.S. Post Office** in **Larimer County, Colorado** ([Wikipedia: Glen Haven, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Haven,_Colorado); [Larimer County — Cities & Towns](https://www.larimer.gov/about/cities-towns)).
- It is located **approximately 7 miles northeast of Estes Park**, situated in Roosevelt National Forest east of Rocky Mountain National Park ([The Thompson Group: Glen Haven](https://estesparkhome.com/communities/glen-haven/)).
- Access is via **Larimer County Road 43** (locally called Devil's Gulch Road or Glen Haven Road), which runs from Estes Park through Glen Haven and down to Drake, terminating at US Highway 34 ([The Thompson Group: Glen Haven](https://estesparkhome.com/communities/glen-haven/); [New Roots Real Estate: Glen Haven](https://newrootsrealestate.com/blog/glen-haven-co-quiet-mountain-living-near-estes-park)). CR 43 is described as the **only vehicle route in and out of Glen Haven** ([New Roots Real Estate](https://newrootsrealestate.com/blog/glen-haven-co-quiet-mountain-living-near-estes-park)).
- Typical drive time to Estes Park is **6–8 miles / 15–20 minutes** under normal conditions ([New Roots Real Estate: Glen Haven](https://newrootsrealestate.com/blog/glen-haven-co-quiet-mountain-living-near-estes-park)); another source states it's "not too far" and roughly 12 miles from RMNP and 36 miles from Fort Collins ([Islands.com: Glen Haven feature](https://www.islands.com/2161297/glen-haven-colorado-between-fort-collins-aspen-western-time-capsule-general-store-mountain-views/)).
- Elevation is reported as **7,200–7,218 feet** ([New Roots Real Estate](https://newrootsrealestate.com/blog/glen-haven-co-quiet-mountain-living-near-estes-park); [The Mountaineer/themtnear.com](https://www.themtnear.com/stories/the-heavens-in-glen-haven,8885)).
- Fox Creek and West Creek join the **North Fork of the Big Thompson River** near downtown Glen Haven, which then flows through Devil's Gulch, receives Miller Fork, and joins the main Big Thompson River at Drake ([The Thompson Group: Glen Haven](https://estesparkhome.com/communities/glen-haven/)).
- The junction of US-34 with the road to Devil's Gulch/Glen Haven is documented at milepost 62.081 on US-34 in Estes Park ([Wikipedia: U.S. Route 34 in Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_34_in_Colorado)).

### Population / character
- Population figures vary by source and are **not from a formal Census count** (Glen Haven has never been tabulated as a CDP in the sources reviewed):
  - **"Roughly 130 inhabitants year-round"** — [The Mountaineer, April 2022](https://www.themtnear.com/stories/the-heavens-in-glen-haven,8885), which also cites Census Bureau geography stating Glen Haven covers 5.9 square miles (5.8 sq mi land, 0.1 sq mi water).
  - **"Estimated population of under 200 residents"** — [Islands.com feature](https://www.islands.com/2161297/glen-haven-colorado-between-fort-collins-aspen-western-time-capsule-general-store-mountain-views/).
  - **"A 400-person jewel box"** (referring to a pre-2013-flood population estimate) — [Coyote Gulch blog, citing 2013 reporting](https://coyotegulch.blog/2013/10/18/slow-recovery-for-glen-haven-drake-coflood/).
  - **"Very small population and a high degree of second-home ownership"** per county materials and local studies — [Estes Park Living, July 2026](https://estesparkliving.com/blog/weekend-living-in-glen-haven-mountain-country).
  - **Given the wide range (130–400+) across non-Census sources, report as: "estimated year-round population in the low hundreds, with a significant share of second homes/vacation cabins" and cite the range** rather than a single figure. **Flag any single precise population number as approximate/non-Census-sourced.**
- Character: a small mountain resort/canyon community that began as a **timber base in the late 1800s** and became a **summer resort starting in 1903** ([Islands.com feature](https://www.islands.com/2161297/glen-haven-colorado-between-fort-collins-aspen-western-time-capsule-general-store-mountain-views/)). It is known for its historic **General Store** (famous for cinnamon rolls, seasonal hours generally Memorial Day–fall), a Town Hall, post office, fire station, trading post, and **The Inn of Glen Haven** bed and breakfast ([The Thompson Group: Glen Haven](https://estesparkhome.com/communities/glen-haven/); [The Mountaineer](https://www.themtnear.com/stories/the-heavens-in-glen-haven,8885); [Islands.com](https://www.islands.com/2161297/glen-haven-colorado-between-fort-collins-aspen-western-time-capsule-general-store-mountain-views/)).
- Governance: Glen Haven has a **Glen Haven Town Board** (5 elected members, 4-year terms, elections in odd years) that meets monthly, but this is a community/advisory body, not a legal municipal government — Glen Haven remains legally unincorporated and relies on Larimer County for law enforcement (Sheriff's Office), elections administration, and vital records ([Public Record Center: Glen Haven](https://www.publicrecordcenter.com/glen_haven_colorado_public_records.html)). Because it is unincorporated, Glen Haven "has no tax base" and "no income generating opportunities" of its own, according to a community leader quoted after the 2013 flood ([CBS News, 2021 retrospective](https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/glen-haven-town-hall-flooding/)).

### School district
- Glen Haven is explicitly named by the **Estes Park School District R-3** as part of its coverage area, alongside Drake, Allenspark, and Pinewood Springs ([Estes Park School District — Our District](https://www.estesschools.org/our-district)).

### Big Thompson River, 1976 flood, 2013 flood, Cameron Peak Fire
- **1976 flood:** The flood caused "extensive damage to buildings in Glen Haven" ([Wikipedia: 1976 Big Thompson River flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Big_Thompson_River_flood)). Locally, the flood is documented to have **moved the Glen Haven town hall several feet off its foundation** ([Glen Haven Association](https://glenhavenassociation.com/)). Glen Haven was rebuilt following the 1976 flood ([Islands.com feature](https://www.islands.com/2161297/glen-haven-colorado-between-fort-collins-aspen-western-time-capsule-general-store-mountain-views/)).
- **2013 flood:** This was catastrophic for Glen Haven specifically — **approximately 80% of Glen Haven's downtown was destroyed** ([Glen Haven Association](https://glenhavenassociation.com/); corroborated by a contemporaneous account stating "an estimated 80 percent of the homes are empty and every business downtown is closed or totally gone" — [Coyote Gulch blog, Oct. 2013](https://coyotegulch.blog/2013/10/18/slow-recovery-for-glen-haven-drake-coflood/)).
- Access to Glen Haven was **completely cut off** when Larimer County Road 43 was destroyed on both sides of town; the segment connecting to Drake and US-34 reopened on **December 6, 2013** ([Glen Haven Association](https://glenhavenassociation.com/)).
- Glen Haven was named among the hardest-hit communities in the entire 2013 Colorado flood event, alongside Jamestown, Lyons, Longmont, Estes Park, and Evans ([Colorado DHSEM After Action Report](https://dhsem.colorado.gov/sites/dhsem/files/documents/Colorado%202013%20Floods%20and%20Black%20Forest%20Fire_%20AAR_IP_Final_6_30_15%20(1).pdf); [The Coloradoan, 2022](https://www.coloradoan.com/story/news/2022/09/09/colorado-2013-flood-anniversary-look-back-estes-park-devastation/67929925007/)).
- More than three years after the 2013 flood, Glen Haven was still working to rebuild its town hall, a $500,000 project the community struggled to fund because, as an unincorporated area, it has no tax base and residents' flood losses were uninsured in many cases ([CBS News Colorado, Aug. 2021](https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/glen-haven-town-hall-flooding/)). A 10th-anniversary community gathering at the rebuilt town hall was reported by public radio in September 2023 ([KUNC, Sept. 2023](https://www.kunc.org/news/2023-09-11/after-devastating-2013-floods-glen-haven-locals-gather-at-rebuilt-town-hall-to-mark-anniversary)).
- **2020 Cameron Peak Fire:** Glen Haven was under **mandatory evacuation** during the fire's push toward the Big Thompson Canyon/Estes Park area in October 2020 ([CODOT news release, Oct. 16, 2020](https://www.codot.gov/news/2020/october-2020/us34-firerelatedclosure); [Denver7, Oct. 2020](https://www.denver7.com/news/wildfire/more-evacuations-issued-for-cameron-peak-fire-as-wind-picks-up-Friday-afternoon); [Wikipedia: Cameron Peak fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Peak_fire)). Evacuation orders specifically named **"Glen Haven, Glen Haven Retreat, Storm Mountain and Palisade Mountain"** as mandatory-evacuation zones ([Denver7, Oct. 2020](https://www.denver7.com/news/wildfire/more-evacuations-issued-for-cameron-peak-fire-as-wind-picks-up-Friday-afternoon)). Power was cut to the Cedar Park area near Glen Haven at the request of fire incident command ([KUNC, Oct. 18, 2020](https://www.kunc.org/2020-10-18/colorado-wildfire-updates-week-oct-14-18)). Structure-protection work occurred at "the far end of the Retreat near the Miller Fork/Copper Hill intersection," with fire reaching Bulwark Ridge but not the road of the same name ([Colorado Sun, Oct. 17, 2020](https://coloradosun.com/2020/10/17/winds-fuel-cameron-peak-fires-growth-toward-fort-collins-more-evacuations-ordered/)). No deaths were reported in the fire overall ([Colorado Encyclopedia: Cameron Peak Fire](https://coloradoencyclopedia.org/article/cameron-peak-fire)).
- Glen Haven's location "in a narrow valley puts the area at risk for flooding," per the community's own association website, which explicitly discusses both the 1976 and 2013 floods as defining events in local history ([Glen Haven Association](https://glenhavenassociation.com/)).

## 4. BIG THOMPSON CANYON

### Basic geography
- Big Thompson Canyon is not itself an incorporated town or CDP — it is a **geographic canyon corridor** along **U.S. Highway 34** that runs approximately **25 miles between Loveland and Estes Park** in **Larimer County, Colorado**, containing the unincorporated communities of Drake, Glen Comfort, Waltonia, Cedar Cove, and others along the way ([AllTrips: Big Thompson Canyon](https://www.allestespark.com/scenic_drives/big_thompson_canyon.php); [Islands.com: Big Thompson Canyon scenic drive](https://www.islands.com/1769482/big-thompson-river-canyon-colorado-scenic-drive-breathtaking-rocky-mountains/)).
- The canyon was carved by the **Big Thompson River**, a 78-mile waterway originating in Rocky Mountain National Park ([Islands.com scenic drive feature](https://www.islands.com/1769482/big-thompson-river-canyon-colorado-scenic-drive-breathtaking-rocky-mountains/); [Wikipedia: Big Thompson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Thompson_River)).
- The drive through the canyon on US-34 is commonly described as taking about **40 minutes one-way for the full 25-mile length** ([AllTrips](https://www.allestespark.com/scenic_drives/big_thompson_canyon.php)); other sources give a Loveland–Estes Park distance/time of **30 miles / 45–55 minutes** ([Wanderlog](https://wanderlog.com/drive/between/58470/58310/loveland-to-estes-park-drive); [Travelmath](https://www.travelmath.com/distance/from/Loveland,+CO/to/Estes+Park,+CO); [Trippy](https://www.trippy.com/distance/Loveland-CO-to-Estes-Park)).
- The canyon begins at **"The Dam Store"** west of Loveland on US-34 ([Roc Doc Travel geology feature](https://www.rocdoctravel.com/2023/04/httpswww.rocdoctravel.comcentral-colorado.html)) and the bedrock is largely metamorphic schist and gneiss; this impermeable rock and the large upstream drainage area around Estes Park is cited by a geology source as the reason the canyon is especially prone to flash flooding ([Roc Doc Travel](https://www.rocdoctravel.com/2023/04/httpswww.rocdoctravel.comcentral-colorado.html)).
- Eight miles west of Loveland, the canyon passes through a notably narrow stretch called **"The Narrows"** where there is only room for two traffic lanes and the river ([MESalek.com: Colorado US 34](https://www.mesalek.com/colo/us34.html)).
- The canyon opens at Estes Park near **Lake Estes and its dam** ([Roc Doc Travel](https://www.rocdoctravel.com/2023/04/httpswww.rocdoctravel.comcentral-colorado.html)).
- Fort Collins to Rocky Mountain National Park via US-34 through Big Thompson Canyon is **41 miles / about 75 minutes**, per a current travel guide, due to the two-lane canyon road and periodic construction delays ([Ridearion RMNP transportation guide, 2026](https://ridearion.com/blog/rocky-mountain-national-park-getting-there-and-around/)).

### Communities along the canyon / character
- Communities in the canyon include Drake and Glen Comfort (each historically cited with populations of "about 200 people" in one travel-guide description) ([AllTrips](https://www.allestespark.com/scenic_drives/big_thompson_canyon.php)), plus smaller settlements Waltonia, Cedar Cove, and Midway that were named in flood accounts ([BizWest, 2026](https://bizwest.com/2026/07/26/disasters-50th-anniversary-brings-flood-of-memories/); [Wikipedia: 1976 Big Thompson River flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Big_Thompson_River_flood)).
- Larimer County operates four free day-use parks along the canyon (west to east from Estes Park): **Sleepy Hollow Park** (in Drake), **Forks Park**, **Narrows Park**, and **Glade Park** — all offering river access for fishing and picnicking, open April–October, no entrance fee ([Larimer County: Big Thompson Parks](https://www.larimer.gov/naturalresources/parks/big-thompson)). These parks were acquired by the county after the 1976 flood specifically to preserve public river access ([Larimer County: Big Thompson Parks](https://www.larimer.gov/naturalresources/parks/big-thompson)).
- The character overall is **rural/mountain canyon residential** — a mix of full-time residences, vacation cabins, guest ranches (e.g., the historic Sylvan Dale Guest Ranch, founded well before 1976 and damaged in both the 1976 and 2013 floods) ([BizWest, 2026](https://bizwest.com/2026/07/26/disasters-50th-anniversary-brings-flood-of-memories/)), and small tourism-oriented businesses.

### 1976 flood, 2013 flood, Cameron Peak Fire — canyon-wide
(See detailed, cited figures under Drake and Glen Haven sections above — these events affected the entire canyon corridor, not just individual communities.)
- Canyon-wide, the 1976 flood destroyed or badly damaged **nearly 10 miles of the highway's 23 miles, and badly damaged another 11 miles**; **42 bridges were lost** (18 along the Big Thompson River, 24 along the North Fork); repairs were estimated at **$16.5 million** at the time. US-34 reopened temporarily in **November 1976** and was not permanently/fully reopened until **May 17, 1980** — nearly four years after the flood ([Jacobs/NAEP Historic Preservation report — "The River and the Road"](https://visualmedia.jacobs.com/NAEP/pdf/Panel_3/Historic%20Preservation%20-%20The%20River%20and%20the%20Road.pdf)).
- The 1976 flood generated an estimated peak discharge of approximately **31,200 cubic feet per second (cfs)** at the mouth of the canyon, and the river's typical depth of about 18 inches surged into a wall of water up to **30 feet high** ([Big Thompson Watershed Coalition Master Plan](https://bigthompson.co/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/BTWC_Master_Plan.pdf); [Rocky Mountain PBS](https://www.rmpbs.org/news/history/big-thompson-flood-50-years-later); [Jacobs/NAEP report](https://visualmedia.jacobs.com/NAEP/pdf/Panel_3/Historic%20Preservation%20-%20The%20River%20and%20the%20Road.pdf)). River levels in 2013 reached or exceeded the 1976 flood's peak level for more than 100 hours, though the 2013 event was less intense overall ([Jacobs/NAEP report](https://visualmedia.jacobs.com/NAEP/pdf/Panel_3/Historic%20Preservation%20-%20The%20River%20and%20the%20Road.pdf)).
- Following the 1976 flood, **US-34 was raised by 4 feet** in places to protect it from future flooding ([Islands.com scenic drive feature](https://www.islands.com/1769482/big-thompson-river-canyon-colorado-scenic-drive-breathtaking-rocky-mountains/)).
- Post-2013, engineering firm Jacobs led a permanent resiliency redesign of **23 miles of US-34**, incorporating a dedicated **15-foot-wide emergency access lane** intended to keep at least minimal ingress/egress open during future flood events ([Jacobs project page](https://www.jacobs.com/projects/us-34-big-thompson-canyon-permanent-repair)). Total reconstruction cost for the permanent US-34 repair project was **$280 million** ([CODOT project archive](https://www.codot.gov/projects/archives/us-34-big-thompson-canyon)).
- The City of Loveland's hydroelectric infrastructure in the canyon (rebuilt after 1976) was again destroyed in 2013: the Idylwilde Reservoir was filled with silt/rock, the Idylwilde Dam broke free of bedrock, and the associated hydroelectric plant was flooded; the dam was ultimately demolished rather than relicensed ([Wikipedia: Big Thompson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Thompson_River)).
- Also destroyed/damaged in 2013: the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation's **Dille Diversion Dam** ([Wikipedia: Big Thompson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Thompson_River)).
- **Cameron Peak Fire (2020):** As detailed above under Drake/Glen Haven, the fire's push toward Big Thompson Canyon in mid-October 2020 triggered a full closure of US-34 (Mall Road in Estes Park to east of the Dam Store) for evacuations, with mandatory evacuations covering Drake-to-Dam-Store segments and Glen Haven, and voluntary evacuation zones extending further down-canyon ([CODOT news release, Oct. 16, 2020](https://www.codot.gov/news/2020/october-2020/us34-firerelatedclosure); [Denver7, Oct. 2020](https://www.denver7.com/news/wildfire/more-evacuations-issued-for-cameron-peak-fire-as-wind-picks-up-Friday-afternoon)).

### Real estate character (canyon-wide)
- **Well/septic:** Standard infrastructure for unincorporated canyon properties; no municipal water/sewer utility serving the canyon corridor was identified in any source reviewed. **Flag as UNVERIFIED for an exact percentage — recommend citing this as general practice, not a hard statistic, unless a Larimer County Health Department source is located.**
- **Wildfire insurance:** As detailed under Drake, Larimer County mountain/canyon properties are widely reported to face wildfire-driven insurance non-renewals from major national carriers, with the Colorado FAIR Plan (launched April 2025) as a last resort for otherwise-uninsurable properties; typical mountain-area premiums are cited in secondary industry sources at roughly **$3,200–$7,500/year** for 2026 ([Colorado DOI Wildfire Preparedness Guide](https://doi.colorado.gov/sites/doi/files/documents/Colorado%20Property%20and%20Insurance%20Wildfire%20Preparedness%20Guide.pdf); [HomeOfferNinja industry analysis](https://homeofferninja.com/colorado-wildfire-insurance-crisis-2026) — cite the latter cautiously as a non-governmental/aggregator source).
- **Winter road conditions:** US-34 through the canyon has repeatedly undergone extended, multi-month winter construction closures in recent years (2016–17, 2017–18) tied to post-flood permanent repairs, during which canyon residents required CDOT-issued access permits with restricted travel windows; the corridor is also subject to periodic rockfall/rockslide mitigation closures (e.g., a landslide-related closure near milepost 72–74 in 2017–18, and brief rock-scaling closures as recently as 2025) ([CODOT news release, Aug. 2017](https://www.codot.gov/news/2017-news/august/us-34-from-estes-park-to-loveland-to-close-oct-2); [CPR, 2016](https://www.cpr.org/2016/09/29/brace-yourself-northern-colorado-us-34-big-thompson-canyon-closure-is-almost-here/); [Estes Valley Voice, 2025](https://estesvalleyvoice.com/2025/05/31/big-thompson-canyon-road-work-this-week/)). Signage throughout the canyon warns of flash-flood danger due to its narrow, steep profile ([AARoads: US 34 Colorado](https://www.aaroads.com/guides/us-034-co)).
- **Property types:** A mix of riverfront cabins, larger acreage parcels (including multi-acre parcels bordering National Forest land), and mountain-view homes on the shoulders of features like Storm Mountain; representative listings found in current MLS-linked sources show land parcels from roughly 1–35+ acres and homes ranging from small A-frame cabins to larger single-family homes ([Zillow: 80517 Big Thompson River listings](https://www.zillow.com/estes-park-co-80517/big-thompson-river_att/); [Hayden Outdoors: Big Thompson Riverfront Mountain Hideaway listing](https://www.haydenoutdoors.com/land-for-sale/big-thompson-riverfront-mountain-hideaway/); [Redfin: 80515 listings](https://www.redfin.com/zipcode/80515); [Trulia: 80517 land listings](https://www.trulia.com/CO/Estes_Park/80517/LOT%7CLAND_type/)).
- Defensible-space and fire-mitigation work is frequently recommended/required by insurers for canyon properties, particularly steep lots dense with gambel oak and ponderosa pine, which can cost "a few hundred dollars" on flatter foothill lots up to "thousands" on steep, heavily forested canyon lots requiring forestry mulching ([Fire Mitigation Experts: Loveland/Big Thompson Canyon service area page](https://firemitigationexperts.com/service-areas/loveland)). Colorado offers a wildfire-mitigation income tax credit (25% of qualifying costs, up to $625/year) and Larimer County residents may access Colorado State Forest Service cost-share grants ([Fire Mitigation Experts](https://firemitigationexperts.com/service-areas/loveland)).

### Zip codes
- The canyon spans multiple ZIP codes depending on location: **80537** (Loveland, east end), **80515** (Drake, mid-canyon), and **80517** (Estes Park, west end) ([Larimer County — Big Thompson Parks addresses](https://www.larimer.gov/naturalresources/parks/big-thompson); [Wikipedia: Drake, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake,_Colorado); [Zillow 80517 listings](https://www.zillow.com/estes-park-co-80517/big-thompson-river_att/)).

### Recreation / distance to Estes Park and RMNP
- Estes Park sits at the western (upper) end of the canyon; the drive from Loveland is **~30 miles / 45–55 minutes** via US-34 ([Wanderlog](https://wanderlog.com/drive/between/58470/58310/loveland-to-estes-park-drive); [Travelmath](https://www.travelmath.com/distance/from/Loveland,+CO/to/Estes+Park,+CO)). An alternate Loveland-to-Estes routing via I-25 and US-36 takes about **65 minutes / 50.5 miles**, while a routing via CO-287 to US-36 takes about **59 minutes / 45 miles** — both slower than the direct canyon route via US-34 ([Visit Estes Park: Highway 34 information page](https://www.visitestespark.com/partners/important-news-information/highway-34/)).
- **RMNP entrances:** The **Fall River Entrance Station** (for Trail Ridge Road) sits on US-34 about 4 miles west of Estes Park; the **Beaver Meadows Entrance Station** (main east entrance, park headquarters) is 3.5 miles west of Estes Park on US-36 ([NPS: Entrance Stations and Visitor Centers](https://www.nps.gov/romo/entrance_stations.htm)).
- From Fort Collins, the NPS itself does not list a direct mileage via the canyon route, but a current third-party RMNP transportation guide cites **41 miles / ~75 minutes** via US-34 through Big Thompson Canyon ([Ridearion RMNP transportation guide, 2026](https://ridearion.com/blog/rocky-mountain-national-park-getting-there-and-along/) — note: cited as [Ridearion RMNP guide](https://ridearion.com/blog/rocky-mountain-national-park-getting-there-and-around/)).
- Trail Ridge Road (the continuation of US-34 through RMNP itself, connecting Estes Park to Grand Lake) is a **seasonal, summer-only road** that closes in winter; it rises to **12,183 feet**, one of the highest paved highway elevations in the U.S. ([AARoads: US 34 Colorado](https://www.aaroads.com/guides/us-034-co); [MESalek.com: Colorado US 34](https://www.mesalek.com/colo/us34.html); [Ridearion RMNP guide](https://ridearion.com/blog/rocky-mountain-national-park-getting-there-and-around/)).
- The canyon and surrounding area is also close to the **Roosevelt National Forest** (Glen Haven vicinity) and **Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forests** more broadly, both offering additional hiking/recreation beyond RMNP itself ([The Thompson Group: Glen Haven](https://estesparkhome.com/communities/glen-haven/); [Islands.com scenic drive feature](https://www.islands.com/1769482/big-thompson-river-canyon-colorado-scenic-drive-breathtaking-rocky-mountains/), which also notes the canyon runs adjacent to the 800,000-acre Roosevelt National Forest).
