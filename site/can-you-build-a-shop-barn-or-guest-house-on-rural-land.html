Can You Build a Shop, Barn, or Guest House on Rural Land?

By Christine Gwinnup · 2025-04-20

Can You Build a Shop, Barn, or Guest House on Rural Land?

If you're buying land in Colorado, one of the most common questions is: Can I build a shop, barn, or even a second dwelling? The answer depends entirely on zoning, setbacks, and the specific county and parcel regulations.

What Zoning Allows Accessory Structures?

In most rural parts of Colorado—especially Larimer, Weld, and surrounding counties—zoning categories such as FA (Farming) or RE (Rural Estate) allow accessory buildings. Common structures include:

  • Detached garages or workshops
  • Metal buildings or pole barns
  • Horse barns or agricultural shelters
  • Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) or guest houses

Key Restrictions to Keep in Mind

Always check with the county planning department before assuming what you can build. A shop or barn on one lot may not be allowed just a mile down the road.
  • Setbacks: Most accessory structures must follow setback rules from property lines.
  • Height limits: These vary by zone and purpose of the building.
  • Building permits: Required for nearly all permanent structures.
  • Use: Some zones prohibit living quarters in accessory buildings without special review.

Guest Houses & ADUs

Accessory Dwelling Units are allowed in some rural zones, but restrictions apply. Factors include:

  • Minimum lot size
  • Septic system capacity and water availability
  • Separate utility connections or shared services

Some counties allow detached guest homes only on parcels above a certain acreage, while others require conditional use approval.

Next Steps

Before you design that dream workshop or barn, verify:

  • Your property’s zoning code
  • Whether covenants or HOA restrictions apply
  • Fire access and rural building requirements

Check with:

  • Larimer County Planning Department
  • Weld County Planning & Zoning

Horse barn requirements in Colorado: the short version

For most unincorporated Northern Colorado parcels, a horse barn is an accessory agricultural building — generally permitted where zoning allows keeping horses, but still subject to the county's building permit rules, setbacks from property lines and wells, and in some cases size thresholds that change the review level. Whether you need a full building permit can depend on the structure's size and use — counties treat a loafing shed differently than a barn with power, water, and living space — so the county building department's current rules for your parcel are the real authority.

Plan the practical layer too: manure management and drainage matter near wells and streams, and if a future buyer will care about the horse setup (in Northern Colorado, they will), building to what the market expects — safe fencing, water to the barn, all-weather access — pays you back at resale. If you're buying land with a barn already on it, permit history is a due-diligence question worth asking.

Guest houses and ADUs: when a second dwelling is allowed

A shop or barn is an accessory building; a guest house someone sleeps in is a DWELLING, and counties regulate those much more closely. Whether your parcel can have one — as a guest house, a full accessory dwelling unit, or not at all — comes down to its zone district's rules on second dwellings, minimum parcel sizes, and sometimes whether the unit can be rented. Water matters here too: a household-use well permit may not cover a second dwelling, which surprises a lot of would-be builders.

The pattern for all of it: your county's land use code plus a conversation with Planning, parcel number in hand, before money moves. If the ability to build a shop, barn, or guest quarters is the REASON you're buying a piece of land, make it a contingency — I write land contracts that way so my buyers aren't betting the purchase on an answer we could have gotten first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I build a shop on my property in Colorado?

On most rural parcels yes, as a permitted accessory building — subject to setbacks, size rules, and a building permit depending on the structure. Confirm with your county's building department using your parcel number; rules differ between counties and zone districts.

What are the requirements for a horse barn in Colorado?

Zoning that allows horses, compliance with setbacks (including from wells), and building-permit requirements that scale with the barn's size and features. Counties publish these rules; verifying them for your parcel takes one call and beats guessing.

Can I build a guest house on my property?

Only if your parcel's zoning allows a second dwelling and its water situation supports one — guest houses and ADUs are regulated as dwellings, not sheds. Check the zone district rules and your well permit type before planning.

Do barns and shops add property value?

In Northern Colorado's rural market, well-built and properly permitted outbuildings are genuine value — especially horse setups done to market expectations. Unpermitted structures do the opposite at sale time, when they become the buyer's inspection issue.
