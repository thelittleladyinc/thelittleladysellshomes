First-Time Home Buyers in Northern Colorado: Programs, Path, and a Guide Who's Done This a Hundred Times

The myth that keeps renters renting

The single biggest barrier for first-time buyers in Northern Colorado isn't prices — it's the belief that you need 20% down. You don't. Conventional first-time programs commonly start around 3% down, FHA at 3.5%, and VA and USDA loans at zero for those who qualify — and that's before any down payment assistance is layered on. What actually decides whether you can buy is the monthly payment you can carry and the shape your credit is in — both of which can be worked on, starting now.

Christine has helped many first-time buyers purchase using down payment assistance — and she puts $500 toward closing costs for her first-time buyers as well. Whether it's enough to change your timeline is a fifteen-minute conversation: start it here or call 303-709-4262.

Down payment assistance and CHFA, in plain English

Colorado's CHFA programs pair a normal 30-year loan with help for the down payment — sometimes a grant you don't repay, sometimes a silent second loan that rides along until you sell or refinance. There are income limits, a homebuyer education class, and trade-offs (assistance usually costs a little in rate), but for buyers whose obstacle is the down payment rather than the monthly payment, it's often the right trade. Program terms and amounts change; treat every specific number you read online as 'verify with your lender this week.'

There are also profession-specific paths — teachers ask Christine about this constantly, and there are programs aimed at educators worth checking before you assume you don't qualify. She keeps a current breakdown on her special buyer programs page, including the teacher grant options.

And if your credit needs work first, that's a plan, not a rejection. Christine will tell you honestly where you stand, what to fix in what order, and check back with you along the way. Renters who think they can't qualify at all should take the NoCo Homeownership Score quiz at OwnInNoCo.com — it exists precisely for the 'not yet' cases.

Where a first budget goes furthest in Northern Colorado

Part of first-time buying here is picking the right town, not just the right house. Greeley offers the region's most accessible pricing with a university-town economy; Eaton, Ault, and Milliken trade a short drive for meaningfully more house; Wellington does the same north of Fort Collins; and Johnstown sits in the I-25 growth corridor where starter neighborhoods keep being built. Christine hosts first-time homebuyer seminars covering exactly this path — pre-approval, credit scores, finding the right home, negotiating, and closing — and her free First-Time Homebuyer Guide walks through it on paper. Frequently Asked Questions

How much do I really need for a down payment in Colorado?

Commonly 3% (conventional first-time programs) or 3.5% (FHA); zero for qualifying VA and USDA buyers; and down payment assistance can cover part of even those amounts. The 20% figure only decides whether you pay mortgage insurance, not whether you can buy. Verify current program terms with a lender — they change.

What credit score do I need to buy my first home?

It depends on the loan type and the lender, and the cutoffs shift — which is why the honest answer is a pre-approval conversation, not a number from the internet. If your score isn't there yet, Christine will help you build the fix-it plan and stay in touch until it is.

Are there home buying programs for teachers in Colorado?

Yes — educator-focused programs exist, and Christine keeps the current options (including grant programs) on her special buyer programs page. Income limits and amounts change, so verify the current terms before counting on a specific figure.

How long does buying a first home take?

Pre-approval takes days; the search takes as long as it takes; once under contract, closing typically runs about a month. Renters on a lease can time all of this — tell Christine your lease end date and she'll work the calendar backward with you.

Do I need a Realtor as a first-time buyer, and what does it cost me?

You need one more than any other kind of buyer — first purchases are where expensive mistakes hide. Agent compensation is negotiated in the transaction; in many Northern Colorado deals the seller's side contributes to it. Christine explains exactly how it works in your deal before you sign anything.
