Rent‑to‑Own vs. Buying: What Northern Colorado Home Shoppers Need to Know

By Christine Gwinnup · 2025-08-12

 Christine Gwinnup, The Little Lady Sells Homes

What Is a Rent-to-Own Home?

A rent-to-own agreement combines a lease with the option (or obligation) to buy the property later. You pay an option fee—typically 1–5% of the purchase price—plus above-market rent. A portion of that rent is credited toward your down payment.

Some landlords require much higher upfront amounts—10–20% or more of the purchase price. These large fees are non-refundable, meaning you could lose all that money if you decide not to purchase or can’t qualify for a mortgage.

Two Types of Agreements

  • Lease-Option: You have the option to buy at the end of the lease but aren’t obligated. If you walk away, you forfeit the option fee and rent credits.

  • Lease-Purchase: You must buy the home at the end of the lease term—risking legal consequences if you can’t complete the purchase.

Pros of Rent-to-Own

  • Time to Improve Credit: Opportunity to build credit before applying for a mortgage.

  • Locked-In Price: Purchase price set at the beginning of the lease.

  • Test Drive the Home: Live in the home before committing to buy.

  • Build Some Equity: Portion of rent may be credited toward the down payment.

The Risks

  • High Upfront Fees: Typical is 1–5%, but some require 10–20%—all non-refundable.

  • Higher Monthly Payments: Above-market rent, with only a portion going toward your purchase.

  • Loss of All Payments: If you walk away, you lose both the option fee and rent credits.

  • Limited Control: You can’t make major changes until you own the home.

  • Contractual Traps: Lease-purchase agreements can lock you into a deal even if your finances change.

  • Potential Scams: Some agreements are poorly structured or offered by sellers without clear title.

A Better Path: CHFA and First-Time Buyer Programs

The Colorado Housing and Finance Authority (CHFA) offers programs that can make buying outright easier and more affordable than a rent-to-own agreement.

  • Down Payment Assistance Grant: Up to 3% of your first mortgage amount—no repayment required.

  • Second Mortgage Loan: Up to 4% of your first mortgage amount, deferred until you sell or refinance.

  • Flexible Loan Types: FHA, VA, USDA, and conventional options with low down payment requirements.

I’ve helped clients use CHFA programs and walk away from the closing table with money back because assistance exceeded their required down payment and closing costs.

Other Low-Down-Payment Options
Learn more HERE

  • FHA Loans: As little as 3.5% down.

  • VA Loans: Zero down for eligible veterans.

  • USDA Loans: Zero down in qualifying rural areas.

  • Local Grants & Buyer Assistance: City and county programs with forgivable second mortgages.

Why Buying Often Beats Rent-to-Own

  • Build Equity Faster: Every mortgage payment increases your ownership stake.

  • Stable Payments: Fixed-rate loans protect you from rent hikes.

  • Full Control: Renovate, rent out rooms, or sell when you choose.

  • Possible Tax Benefits: Deduct mortgage interest and property taxes.

Before You Sign Anything

Always have a real estate attorney review a rent-to-own agreement to ensure the terms are fair, the property has clear title, and you fully understand your rights and obligations.
