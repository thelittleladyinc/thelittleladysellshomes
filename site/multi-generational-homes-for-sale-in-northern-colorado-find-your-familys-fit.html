What are 'Next Gen' homes?

'Next Gen' is builder language — popularized as a trademarked 'home within a home' design — for a floor plan with a semi-independent suite: typically its own entrance, bedroom, living space, kitchenette, and laundry inside the main home. Other builders sell the same idea under names like multi-gen, dual living, or casita plans. In Northern Colorado's newer communities around Windsor, Severance, Timnath, and Greeley, these plans appear regularly in new construction.

On the resale market the same function hides under different labels: in-law suite, mother-in-law quarters, walkout basement with second kitchen, carriage house, or a property with a permitted ADU. That's why searching only the phrase 'next gen homes for sale' misses most of the real inventory — the right search is by FEATURES, which is exactly how I set up my clients' searches.

What to check before buying a multi-generational home

Function first: a true multi-gen setup needs real separation — its own entrance and bath at minimum, ideally kitchen facilities and laundry — plus accessibility if aging parents are part of the plan: a main-floor suite, minimal steps, wide doorways. Half-finished 'could be a suite' spaces are negotiating points, not solutions.

Rules second: if the second kitchen or separate unit matters to your plan, confirm how it's classified — some towns and HOAs regulate ADUs and rentals differently than an attached suite used by family. I check this during showings so a family doesn't fall in love with a layout the rules won't let them use as intended. Start with the multi-generational buyer's guide, then search current Northern Colorado listings and tell me who the home needs to hold — I'll build the feature search that finds it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Next Gen home?

A builder floor plan containing a semi-independent suite — separate entrance, living space, kitchenette, often laundry — inside the main house, designed for parents, adult children, or long-term guests. Several Northern Colorado builders offer versions under different names.

Are there multi-generational homes for sale in Colorado right now?

Yes, but they hide under many labels: next gen plans in new construction, in-law suites, basement apartments, and homes with ADUs or guest houses on resale. A feature-based search catches what a keyword search misses — that's how I run them for clients.

Is a mother-in-law suite the same as an ADU?

Not legally. A suite is part of the main dwelling; an ADU is a separately permitted dwelling unit, with rules that vary by town — affecting rentals, utilities, and sometimes resale value. Which one fits depends on how your family will actually use it.

Do multi-generational homes cost more?

Purpose-built plans and homes with true second suites carry a premium over comparable single-suite homes, but shared among two households the math often beats running two separate homes — and Northern Colorado's inventory spans a wide price range. Tell me your combined budget and I'll show you what it buys.

Which Northern Colorado towns are best for multi-generational living?

Newer communities in Windsor, Severance, Timnath, and Greeley carry the most purpose-built plans; Loveland and Fort Collins offer more resale homes with in-law setups and walkout-basement suites. The right town depends on the family's work, schools, and care needs — that's a conversation, and I'm happy to have it.
