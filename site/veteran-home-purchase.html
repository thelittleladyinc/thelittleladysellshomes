What makes a VA loan different — in plain English

The VA loan is the strongest buying tool most veterans own and many never use: typically zero down payment, no monthly mortgage insurance, and rates that routinely price below comparable conventional loans. Instead of mortgage insurance there's a one-time funding fee — and if you receive VA disability compensation, you may be exempt from it entirely. Buying your first home with it? The first-time buyer playbook stacks with everything here. Your lender confirms all of this against your Certificate of Eligibility (COE), which Christine can help you request before you ever tour a home.

With full entitlement there is effectively no VA loan limit anymore — what matters is what the payment math and the appraisal support, not an arbitrary cap. That surprises a lot of buyers who walked away from the benefit years ago and never looked again.

VA home buying in Loveland, Fort Collins, Greeley, and Eaton

Northern Colorado doesn't have a base next door, but it has a deep community of veterans, guard and reserve families, and military retirees — and inventory that fits VA buying well when you know where to look. Loveland and Fort Collins offer the widest range; Greeley and Eaton stretch the same payment meaningfully further; and if a PCS or retirement move is bringing you here from out of state, Christine tours homes by video and handles the ground game until you arrive.

One local wrinkle worth knowing: on rural properties — and Northern Colorado has plenty — wells, septic systems, and outbuildings interact with the VA appraisal's minimum property requirements. Christine's specialty in land and rural property means she flags those issues before you write, not after the appraiser does.

How to write a VA offer sellers actually take

Some listing agents still steer sellers away from VA offers based on myths — slow closings, brutal appraisals, no flexibility. The cure is an agent who packages the offer so those myths never get traction: a strong local lender's pre-approval, realistic timelines the listing side can verify with a phone call, clean terms, and a direct conversation agent-to-agent about what the VA process does and doesn't require. That last step is where Christine's fierce-negotiator reputation earns its keep.

VA rules also allow the seller to pay concessions toward your costs — useful in a market where negotiation has returned. Whether to ask, and how much, is a strategy call made offer by offer, not a default.

The VA appraisal, assumable loans, and using the benefit again

The VA appraisal checks value and the home's basic condition — safe, sound, sanitary. Peeling paint on an older home, missing handrails, or a dodgy heat source can trigger repair conditions; an experienced eye spots these during the showing so they become negotiating items instead of closing-week surprises.

Two more things many veterans never hear: VA loans are assumable — a qualified buyer can take over your loan and its rate when you sell, which can make your home genuinely more valuable to the right buyer — and your entitlement is restorable, so buying with a VA loan now doesn't burn the benefit forever. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a VA loan specialist Realtor?

You need a Realtor who has closed VA purchases recently and often — the loan is handled by your lender, but the offer strategy, appraisal expectations, and listing-agent conversations are your agent's job. Over 25% of Christine's transactions have served veterans and military families across Loveland, Fort Collins, Greeley, and Eaton.

Do sellers avoid VA offers?

Some try to, based on outdated assumptions. A well-packaged VA offer — strong local pre-approval, verified timelines, and an agent who addresses the myths head-on — competes with any financed offer. Christine's job is making sure yours is that offer.

Is there a Colorado property tax break for disabled veterans?

Colorado has a property tax exemption program for qualifying disabled veterans, and surviving spouses may also qualify. The qualification rules and amounts are set by the state and change over time, so treat this as a lead to verify — Christine can point you to the current Larimer or Weld County assessor process.

I'm PCSing or retiring here from out of state. How does that work?

Video tours, honest neighborhood talk (including the parts a listing won't tell you), coordination with your lender's timeline, and an agent on the ground for inspections and walk-throughs.
