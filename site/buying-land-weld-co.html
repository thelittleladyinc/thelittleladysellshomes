Buying Land in Weld County, Colorado

Wells, Septic, Zoning, Utilities & What You Must Know

Weld County offers a wide range of rural land opportunities — but every town, district, and property comes with its own set of rules. Here's your local buyer's guide.

Water Wells in Weld County

Weld County follows the same state permitting system as the rest of Colorado. But enforcement and oversight differ from Larimer.

  • Ask for the well permit number and historical testing results
  • Properties over 35 acres may qualify for broader use (irrigation, livestock)
  • Check for ditch or irrigation rights if near ag fields

Septic Systems in Weld County

Unlike Larimer, a Transfer of Title for septic is NOT automatic in Weld — you must request one if you want the system inspected during the sale.

  • No county-mandated inspection unless you ask for it
  • Always hire a licensed septic pro to inspect & verify capacity
  • Plan on maintenance costs if system hasn’t been serviced recently

Zoning, Setbacks & Land Use

Weld zoning rules are more permissive in some areas, but you still need to verify setbacks, outbuilding allowances, and potential HOA restrictions.

  • Verify zoning through the Weld County Planning Department
  • Setbacks may vary widely by town or subdivision
  • Accessory buildings usually allowed, but confirm use type (ag, residential, etc.)

Weld Zoning & Planning

Utilities & Access

Land in Weld may or may not have utilities close by — and extending service can get expensive. Ask these questions early:

  • Is electric nearby? How many poles are needed to reach the build site?
  • Will you need propane or is natural gas available?
  • Is internet service wired or wireless? Satellite may be the only option

Legal Access, Surveys & Easements

Some rural Weld properties may have unclear access routes. Always verify:

  • Is there a legal recorded easement?
  • Has the land been surveyed recently?
  • Are there shared roads or maintenance agreements?

The Weld County land checklist

Weld County land runs from irrigated farm ground to new-community edge parcels, and the due diligence follows the same spine as anywhere in Northern Colorado — zoning, water, septic, access — with two Weld-specific emphases. First, mineral rights: much of Weld sits over active oil and gas, and knowing what's severed, what's leased, and where any facilities or setbacks fall relative to your building site is fundamental to both value and daily life. Second, water: in a county built on irrigated agriculture, what water (if any) conveys with the land — shares, taps, or well rights — can matter more than the acreage number.

USDA $0-down financing also reaches many Weld towns' surroundings — a real path to acreage for buyers who assume land means cash. The rules on wells, septic, and building mirror what I've written for Larimer County with Weld's own code and health department as the authorities; bring me a parcel and we'll run the checklist against the county's current rules together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Weld County a good place to buy land?

It offers some of Northern Colorado's most attainable acreage, with strong towns like Windsor, Severance, and Eaton growing around it. The value question is parcel-specific — water, minerals, access, and zoning decide it, and all four are checkable before you offer.

What about oil and gas rights on Weld County land?

Assume nothing conveys until title work says otherwise — mineral estates in Weld are commonly severed from the surface. What's beneath, who leases it, and where facilities sit near your parcel belong in your due diligence, and I make sure they're in it.

Can I get USDA zero-down financing for Weld County land?

USDA financing applies to homes in eligible rural areas — including much of Weld outside the bigger towns — rather than bare land alone, and eligibility maps change. If owning acreage with a home is the goal, ask us to verify what's currently eligible; it's a path many buyers don't know they have.
